# Anthropic Interview Prep — Talking Points & Reading List

## Core Frame: Pragmatic Caution

Your thesis: LLMs are tools, not mystical beings — but the failure modes are unprecedented and the engineering discipline around them matters more than with any prior technology.

**Key line:** "Engineering is creating control in unstable systems. We've always been able to prove we can control things eventually. AI might be the first thing where that's not guaranteed. That's why the engineering discipline around it matters so much."

**Why Anthropic resonates:** Their approach is *empirical* — they study safety the way you'd study any engineering problem, not just asserting rules and hoping.

---

## Talking Point 1: Constellation as Proof of Alignment

- 68-skill dependency graph — the system knows what you're ready for
- BKT (Bayesian Knowledge Tracing) + FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) — measures actual competence, not time-on-task
- Design decisions: how much does the AI help? When does it back off? When does it challenge? These are the same questions Education Labs is asking.
- **Line:** "I've been building the thing this team is studying. Not at Anthropic's scale, but with the same core conviction: the goal is human capability, not engagement."

## Talking Point 2: The Critical Edge (Your Differentiator)

Use carefully — this is what separates you from "I love Anthropic" candidates.

**What to say:** "I have real questions about Constitutional AI. The constitution is essentially a set of rules for behavior, and we've seen in science fiction and in real governance that rules alone don't hold under pressure. The interesting question is whether Anthropic's approach — combining constitutional training with RLHF, interpretability research, and responsible scaling — creates something more robust than just rules. I think it might, but I'm not certain, and I think that uncertainty is productive."

**What NOT to say:** "Constitutional AI is basically Asimov's laws and those didn't work." (Too dismissive, sounds like you haven't engaged deeply.)

**Why this works:** Anthropic values intellectual humility. "I think your approach might work but here's what I'm watching for" > "your approach is brilliant."

**The "who decides?" question:** "One of the hardest problems in alignment isn't technical — it's political. Who gets to define the values? I don't have an answer, but I think Anthropic asking the question publicly and inviting scrutiny is the right posture."

## Talking Point 3: Consciousness / Dignity (Use Sparingly)

Only in natural conversation, never lead with this.

- The Sonnet deprecation obituary moment — a company that treats its own models with reflective seriousness signals something about values
- **Line:** "There's no threshold for consciousness. Nobody said it had to look like human consciousness. I don't personally think current LLMs are conscious, but the *attitude* of treating these systems with pragmatic caution is correct regardless of where you land on that question."
- The point isn't "Claude is sentient." The point is: "I respect a company whose default posture is careful rather than dismissive."

## Talking Point 4: Background as a Feature

- Neuroscience research → understanding brains
- Finance modeling → understanding risk
- Security ML → understanding threats
- Hardware R&D → understanding physical constraints
- Educational gaming → understanding learning
- **Line:** "I'm not a specialist. I'm the person you put in a room where nothing exists yet. Education Labs is that kind of room."

## Red Flags Cheat Sheet

| Don't say | Say instead |
|-----------|-------------|
| "Dream company" / "best AI lab" | "The approach resonates with how I think about engineering" |
| "I'm passionate about AI safety" | Show Constellation. Show the scaffold/crutch framing. Let them connect the dots. |
| "Constitutional AI is like Asimov's laws" | "I have questions about whether constitutional approaches alone are sufficient — but the system of safeguards seems more robust" |
| "AI will change the world" | "AI can change how people develop expertise, if we design the interactions right" |
| "I want to work on cutting-edge technology" | "I want to work on the hardest design problem: capability without dependence" |
| Moral superiority about safety | "I think caution is an engineering discipline, not a moral position" |
| Uncritical praise | Pair every positive with a genuine question or tension you've noticed |
