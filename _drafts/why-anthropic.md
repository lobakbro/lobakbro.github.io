I build adaptive learning systems. My project Constellation models a learner's knowledge as a graph of skills with prerequisite relationships, uses Bayesian Knowledge Tracing and spaced repetition to estimate where they actually are, and generates practice that targets the frontier — what they're ready to learn next, not what's next on a syllabus. I use it daily. The hardest design problem I've encountered isn't the AI — it's deciding when the system should help, when it should back off, and when it should challenge. That's the same problem Education Labs is working on.

What draws me to Anthropic specifically is the engineering discipline around safety. The Responsible Scaling Policy treats risk the way I'd expect a serious engineering organization to treat it: empirically, with defined evaluations and thresholds, not with assertions and hopes. Constitutional AI is interesting to me not because I think it's a solved problem — I have genuine questions about whether constitutional approaches alone are sufficient under pressure — but because Anthropic pairs it with interpretability research, RLHF, and structured scaling commitments. The system of safeguards is more convincing than any individual component.

I'm also drawn to how this team ships. Claude Code started as a two-person project. That ratio of ambition to headcount tells me something about the culture — that the work is organized around capability, not hierarchy. I work best in environments like that: small teams, ambiguous problem spaces, high trust. At IvyBee I'm the sole backend engineer across the entire platform. Before that I was on a four-person R&D team building an experimental 3D printer from scratch. I don't need structure handed to me — I build it.

The reason I'm applying to Education Labs and not a general engineering role is that I've already been working on this problem independently. Constellation exists because I believe the goal of AI in education should be human capability, not engagement — that the system succeeds when the learner doesn't need it anymore. I'd rather build toward that conviction inside a team that has the research depth, the models, and the mandate to do it at scale.
